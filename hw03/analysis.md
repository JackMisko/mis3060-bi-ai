# HW3 Analysis — Executive Changes vs. Earnings Announcements

Based on my data, executive changes tend to come before earnings announcements rather than after them: of the 38 extracted events, 25 were filed before the nearest earnings release, 10 after, and 3 in the same week. Counting each Item 5.02 filing once, so that a filing naming several people is not counted several times, gives a similar split: 11 before, 7 after and 1 in the same week, out of 19 filings. The pattern varies by company: Walmart (3 of 4 filings before, plus its McMillon-to-Furner CEO transition filed 6 days before its Q3 FY26 earnings), NVIDIA (3 of 5) and JPMorgan (2 of 3) lean toward disclosing before earnings, while Microsoft is the exception, with 3 of its 4 filings coming after earnings, mostly board changes. Apple's largest change, the Tim Cook-to-John Ternus CEO transition, was filed 10 days before its fiscal Q2 2026 results, which fits the idea that companies announce major leadership news ahead of the earnings call rather than letting it overshadow the results. With only 19 filings, and gaps that are usually three to six weeks in a quarterly reporting cycle, the lean toward "before earnings" is suggestive rather than conclusive, and some extracted names are parsing noise (for example, "Compensatory Arrangements" at JPMorgan) that inflate the row counts but do not change the filing-level timing.
